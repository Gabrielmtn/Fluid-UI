Pane width rule

  1. Snap to the 24 px module. A pane's width is its label plus 18 px padding each side, rounded up to the next 24 px. The minimum is 48 px, two modules. Height is always 48 px.
  2. A bank shares one width. Every pane in a radio or checkbox bank takes the width of its widest member, so the bank reads as one strip of tubes.
  3. Indicators take one module. Radio and checkbox panes reserve 24 px on the left for the cathode indicator: a round cathode for a radio, a square one with a check for a checkbox.
  4. The primary action fills its column. Ignite takes whatever width its row leaves; nothing else stretches.
  5. 8 px between panes, 10 px between frames. Copper bezels sit just inside each pane, so an 8 px gap reads as two bezels side by side rather than empty space.

Sidebar pattern rules

  1. Two tiers. Main controls are 48 px tall on the 24 px module. Sidebar and card controls use the compact tier: 32 px tall, snapped to a 16 px module, 9 px labels.
  2. Brightness carries meaning. Unlit means off. A field's current value glows at half strength and rises to full on hover or focus. A lit pane is an active state. A slow breathing pulse means the control is waiting for you, like a keycap listening for a key.
  3. Fields are select-only comboboxes. They fill their column, keep the value left-aligned and put a copper chevron in the last cell. Options open inline below the field, pushing content down rather than covering it. Keyboard behaviour follows the ARIA pattern.
  4. Two-way choices join. Stroke or Time, On move or Constant: one strip with square inner corners and no indicator. Three or more options use indicator panes.
  5. One slider everywhere. Sidebar sliders use the console's bargraph tube: a neon column with a white-hot head, copper diamonds above and below the value, and a dim filament where it's unlit. Stepped settings add copper detent ticks under the tube.
  6. Brushes live on a screen. All eight sit on one cathode screen, four to a row. Each stamp glows in its brush's own colour, with its number top left and its colour source as one letter top right: M, C, R or P. Pick a brush to edit it underneath; its custom brush is chosen on a smaller scrolling screen that holds every stamp, six to a row, glowing in that brush's colour. Brushes beyond the count dim but stay editable.
  7. One container per column. The panel's copper line is the only box. Sections inside it have no border of their own; a bead-row divider opens each one, with a small atom resting in a gap in the beads.
  8. Atoms move only when something changes. Change any setting, or have another player change one, and the atom for that section eases into a short spin and settles back to rest. Under reduced motion they stay still.
  9. The organizer is a screen. Stamps render as teal phosphor; the chosen one is at full brightness with copper corner brackets. Arrow keys move through it as a grid.
  10. Sidebar settings are personal. Brush, multi-brush and input settings aren't shared, so they never report to the cathode screen and other players never change them.

Cathode screen rules

  1. Only shared settings report. Anything that syncs to other players gets a line. Local view state, like opening the drawer, never does.
  2. Your changes report too, briefly. They strike fast in your color and leave after 1.8 s. Other players' lines warm up slowly and stay for 4.5 s.
  3. Newest on top, three lines. Older lines step down and dim to 62% then 40% of full brightness, like cooling tubes. A fourth pushes the oldest off with an afterglow.
  4. One gesture, one line. Changes by the same player to the same setting within 900 ms merge into one line that shows where the value started and where it is now.
  5. Held controls queue. If someone changes a control you're holding, their line reads QUEUED and stays until you let go. Then it shows their value with AFTER YOU.
  6. Screen readers hear other players only. Their changes go to a polite live region, at most one every 3.5 s, always with the newest. Your own changes aren't announced, because you made them.

Inbound changes beyond the console

  1. Shared or personal. The left brush sidebar (brush, multi-brush, input) is yours alone. Everything else syncs: the console and every section in the right sidebar, so all of them show other players' hands.
  2. Every inbound change wears the player's colour. The control glows in their colour and fades back to copper and orange over 1.4 s, a name chip rises above it, and a line posts to the cathode screen.
  3. Tubes tint their neon. While another player drags a slider, its column and copper diamonds take their colour and glide on the slower remote spring.
  4. Gates show who drew them. A new or moved gate lights its lid in the player's colour with their chip at its corner, and moves in short visible steps rather than jumping.
  5. Gravity leaves a trace. Another player's move draws the afterglow trail in their colour, so you see the path they took, not just where the dot ended up.
  6. Hands off what you're holding. If you're dragging a slider, gate or the gravity dot when someone else changes it, their change waits until you let go, then lands with AFTER YOU on the screen.
  7. Collapsed doesn't mean silent. A change inside a closed layer still posts to the screen and spins that layer's atom; the layer never opens on its own.
  8. Never take focus or selection. Inbound changes don't move your keyboard focus, close your open field, or change which gate or brush you have selected.
